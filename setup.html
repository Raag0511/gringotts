GRINGOTTS

Goblins at work

Your own expense tracker, running in your own Google account. Free, about 10 minutes, once.

1Before you start: Google will warn you, and that's expected

Gringotts is a small app that runs inside your own Google account. It isn't on Google's app store, so Google shows warnings for it. Here is what you'll see, and why it's fine:

  • "Google hasn't verified this app." Google shows this for every app that isn't on its store. Tap Advanced, then Go to Gringotts (unsafe). "Unsafe" is Google's standard word for any unverified app, not a finding about this one.
  • A long list of permissions. The app needs each one: it reads your bank SMS into your sheet, reads card statement emails in your Gmail, sends you a morning email, keeps a weekly backup in your Drive, and sets up its own links ("Apps Script projects").
  • Your data stays yours. Everything is kept in your own Google Sheet and Drive. Nothing is sent to anyone else. You can see or remove the app's access any time in your Google Account, under Security, "Your connections to third-party apps".
  • Updates come from the person who shared Gringotts with you, but they can't see your data. Once a day your app checks for a new release they have published and moves to it by itself. They can't open your sheet, and nothing you enter passes through their account. Nobody but them can change the shared code, and you only ever get a finished release. Like any app that updates itself, it works on trust, so it's best set up with someone you know.

2Make your own copy

This copies the Gringotts sheet, and the app inside it, into your Google Drive. Use the Google account you want to keep your expenses in.

Make my copy

The copy link is being prepared. Ask the person who shared Gringotts for it.

3In your copy: Gringotts › Set up

Open your copy. In the menu at the top, tap Gringotts, then Set up. Google asks for permission (step 1 says what you'll see). The app then makes its tabs and its daily checks.

4One switch, then your links

Set up shows a box with a link: turn on Google Apps Script API there, come back, and tap It's on: make my links. The app makes its own links. Then the box shows a QR code and a setup code.

5On your phone

  • Scan the QR code. Gringotts opens with your own link. Add it to your Home screen (Chrome: ⋮ › Add to Home screen; iPhone or iPad Safari: Share › Add to Home Screen).
  • Android: install MacroDroid from the Play Store, import the Gringotts macro, and paste your setup code when it asks. Tap Send a test: Gringotts shows Phone connected ✓.

    Optional but recommended: MacroDroid's free version works, but it needs you to watch an ad now and then to keep running. MacroDroid Pro (about ₹250, once, bought inside MacroDroid) runs without ads. Recommended, not required.

  • iPhone: set up one Shortcuts automation per bank (guide to follow). An iPhone can't read older SMS, so card statements from Gmail fill any gaps.

6Open Gringotts

A Welcome card asks your name and style. Your cards appear by themselves: each one is offered when its first SMS arrives, and its statement layout is learned from its first statement.
